The Structure · not a descent

The Road, not the Deep

You asked for regions like a 5-floor arc — but not deepening-to-deepening. The novel already draws the map: Swan's slave-smuggling corridor. Progression is horizontal. Regions differ in kind — a scarcity rule and a security layer — never in depth.

The Engine · your food system

Hunger is the Clock

Strip out the descent and you lose the dungeon's built-in pressure — unless food becomes it. Your four states aren't a health bar; they're a risk dial. Milek only raids the enemy camp because he's starving. The story proves it: "I'm still hungry. Are you not hungry?"

WELL FED
STABLE
HUNGRY
STARVING

Well-fed → patience is affordable; wait for the real opening.  Hungry → the meter pushes you toward risks you'd never take fed.  Starving → forced hands, lethal & systemic (two children die on the road from being "too far from the windows"). Hunger is information, not emergency — the forest taught Milek to read it, and so does the UI.

The Forge · four lenses

How the ideas were struck

Each lens shifted the domain. The best ideas came from pressing the fiction until it turned into a mechanic.

01

Steal & Twist

mine survival roguelikes — then twist off their axis
  • Regions = legs of a fixed itinerary, not floors. Twist: you don't choose the path — the merchant does.
  • Security re-tunes per region: knights escort to the border, then leave; local mercenaries take over; gray-law towns have no rules at all. The opening lives in the handoff.
  • The title is the pillar — "The Margins": freedom is what you do in the slack, not where you go.
02

First Principles

what is each system FOR in the player's head?
  • The clock is horizontal: "the caravan leaves at dawn" + hunger — never depth.
  • Regions differ in kind: forage-rich wild · lawful town (can't act) · gray-law market · exposed coast · altitude scarcity · prison starvation.
  • Hunger as a resource you read, not a panic bar — spend risk against it deliberately.
03

Morphological Mixing

cross forage × need, region × security, food × bond
  • Forage feeds two tracks: FOOD (hunger) vs CAMP-SUPPLY (survive the night).
  • Wet/dry branches couple weather to survival — dry burns, rain soaks your blanket, forcing the waterproofing sub-goal.
  • Pebbles → your shipped Epic 2 systems: throw to make Noise elsewhere, pull a patrol. Survival & stealth share a currency.
  • The camp raid: sneak an enemy camp over multiple nights, learn who sleeps lightly, greed-curve the loot — bread → rope → blanket → eggs → the waxed canvas.
04

Assumption Reversal

flip a load-bearing belief, see what game falls out
  • You don't escape the convoy — you ride it. "We'll come with you. No resistance." Bank intel; pick the ONE real opening.
  • Hoarding food loses; giving it wins — Henry's bread buys loyalty. The meter you spend buys people, not just calories.
  • You can't kill the boss — Spearshot intercepts the punch before it's thrown. Victory is being uninteresting. Stealth = social invisibility, a "notability" stat beside line-of-sight.
  • Death isn't the fail state — separation is. Losing a companion to the auction hurts more than dying.
Route 1 · already written

The Forest Leg is Chapter 3

Your MVP isn't invented from scratch — it's an adaptation. Eight-year-old Milek and six-year-old Erik flee into the north pines, shelter in a hollow rotted trunk, and do exactly the survival loop you're designing. Your forage table maps 1:1 onto the prose.

Crafting recipes = the chapter's literal to-do list — "we still need fire… and something waterproof."

moss → warmth / bedding· tinder + dry branch → fire· bark + waxy leaves → water collector· stolen canvas → waterproofing

The mechanic no stealth game does

Milek "checks the mushrooms three times" before letting Erik eat — that's identify-on-use as a time-cost + Instinct check, and feeding an unidentified one to a companion is the horror version. Then, on the fifth raid, pushing deepest for the canvas, he gets followed home. So detection isn't an alarm — it's a tail. Get seen and a patient enemy trails you back to your shelter and your sleeping companion. Failure threatens what you protect, not just you. Lose the tail before you dare return to camp.

The Synthesis · the spine

What it all becomes

The Margins is a horizontal survival-stealth roguelike where hunger is the clock, forage & feeding-lines are the loop, detection means being trailed home to those you protect, the merchant fixes the route while you work the margins — and the emotional engine is holding a growing party together against forced separation.

Clock

Hunger, not descent. The road advances; the meter pushes.

Loop

Forage & feed. Two tracks — eat, and survive the night.

Stakes

The tail home. Being seen endangers your companion, not just you.

Agency

The margins. A fixed route; freedom in its slack.

Heart

Reunion. Separation is the fail state; a party is the goal.
