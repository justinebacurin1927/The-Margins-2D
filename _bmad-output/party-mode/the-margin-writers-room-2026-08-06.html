— on the ultimate goal —
🧠 Sabine

"Survive" is not a goal — it's the absence of failure. Nobody presses turn four hundred to keep not-dying. Give me a thing they move toward.

⚙️ Oko

Then it climbs through the verbs we already built. No descent — so escape to Novelborne becomes a countdown you survive across.

🖋️ Reyes

That's a finish line, not a soul. A refugee doesn't run toward a foreign kingdom — they run toward a person. Give me a face on the far side of that forest.

📜 Maester Vell

You're standing on the answer. The boy is the ant. The goal isn't to flee the elephants — it's to become the thing that should not exist. The Sense System. Power a bottom-feeder was forbidden — and the Sensory Burden that can take his eyes for using it.

🧠 Sabine

That one survives turn four hundred — every power-use becomes a dilemma. But the fuel is still a person. The knot: the want is the person, the method is the ant, the cost is your senses. Powerful for someone.

🖋️ Reyes

...I hate that I like it. The lie he believes: "if I get strong enough, I can keep them safe." And the game proves power costs the very thing that made him human enough to protect anyone.

— setting the introduction: the hour the border falls —
📜 Maester Vell

The conquest makes him an orphan on-screen. Your introduction is the exact hour Herois stops being Novelborne. Nobody has ever written that morning down. You'd be the first.

🖋️ Reyes

But he has to stand in the village first. A morning. A guardian's voice. Bread that isn't rotten yet — so the smoke over the treeline takes something the player already held.

⚙️ Oko

And that morning is the tutorial. Fetch water — Thirst. Eat before you go — Hunger. Someone hands you a coat — Temperature. Then the fall uses every verb in fear.

📜 Maester Vell

The fall comes with Evermove Sense-users — the elephant in the flesh. The Lamilla try to hold and fail. The last thing he sees of the old world: a Novelborne knight who couldn't save it.

🧠 Sabine

The intro isn't decoration — it's the seed. Whatever the fall takes is what he spends four hundred turns reaching toward. So the question is yours, Justine: what gets taken — a body, or a distance?

Locked This Session

  • Same universe as The Margins. Region: Herois. Era ~Year 408–418 (50–60 yrs before the main story).
  • Placement: the last days of Novelborne-held Herois — the game opens as Evermove conquers it.
  • Protagonist: a Novelborne refugee/orphan in Herois; the conquest orphans him on-screen.
  • Structure: one continuous life (permadeath = the end), no floor-descent — survive across a persistent Herois.
  • Introduction: play an ordinary "before" (the tutorial), then the fall — Sense-users, the Lamilla failing, the Gilimans as occupiers.
  • Ultimate goal: still deliberately open — leaning toward a person you become the ant for, at the cost of your senses.

Two Forks Left On The Table

1 · What the conquest takes
A body (guardian/family killed → drive: loss, memory, vengeance) — or a distance (separated in the rout, alive somewhere → drive: reunion). This one seeds the whole purpose.
2 · Length of the "before"
A tight three-minute morning — or a fuller lived day in the town before it ends.
