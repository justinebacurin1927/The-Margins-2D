# Prologue — The Fall of Corneo

*Delivery: the game's text-based intro — **read or skip** (see [[Lore System - Storyline Roadmap]] Act 0). Paged screen by screen; hands into gameplay with Klein and [[NPC Roster|Aldric]] fleeing into the forest.*

---

Corneo sat at the far northwest of Herois, where the pines grew so close that morning arrived late and left early, and the fog came down each night like it had nowhere better to be. It was the last Novelborne town before the treeline — the edge of the map, the edge of the region, the edge of anyone's concern. The Copper Road ran through it and kept going, west toward the border and the kingdom beyond, and most days nothing traveled that road but merchants too poor for the Silver one, and rain.

It was, everyone agreed, the safest posting in the kingdom. Nothing ever happened in Corneo. That was the whole point of it.

---

Klein had expected more.

He was four-and-twenty, newly sworn, the son of a mainland house with a name that meant something three hundred miles east and nothing at all out here. He had trained for a war his instructors spoke of as though it might come any season — and instead the crown had handed him a copper road and a sleepy town and told him to keep them. *Guard the road. Guard the town.* Two duties, and neither had ever once required his sword.

He wrote to Magdalene that it was beautiful, in its way. He did not write that he was bored. She would have heard it anyway. She always did.

---

Her last letter had come folded small on the supply cart, smelling faintly of the paper-shop on her father's street. She wrote about the wedding her mother was planning without them. She wrote that his parents had asked after him twice — his father's cough was *no worse*, which in his mother's hand meant it was no better. And at the end, in the smaller script she used when she meant it:

*Come home soon. I have been practicing patience. I find I am not good at it.*

He kept the letter in his coat, against his chest. He meant to answer it that week.

---

The posting had one mercy, and his name was Aldric.

They had sworn the same season and been sent to the same nowhere, and Aldric had decided on the first night that if they were going to rot at the edge of the world they might as well do it laughing. He was the better swordsman and the worse liar, and he had a gift for making a long watch short. When Klein complained about the cold, Aldric told him the cold built character. When Klein complained about the quiet, Aldric told him quiet was a thing he'd learn to miss — which was rich, from a man three months his senior.

"Homesick already," Aldric said, that last morning, watching him tuck the letter away. "You'll marry the girl and bore her half to death with stories about the terrible winter you survived in Corneo."

"There's nothing here to survive," Klein said.

He would remember saying it.

---

The horn came at midmorning.

Not the watch-horn — not the long single note that meant a cart on the road. This was the other one. Short, repeated. A sound Klein had heard exactly once, in training, and been told he would likely never hear for real. He was halfway to the wall before he understood he was already running.

They came from the treeline. Not scouts, not raiders — a column, ordered, under a banner Klein had only ever seen painted in books: the Evermove crown, and beneath it the grey ranks of the **Gilimans**, more of them than Corneo had souls. The garrison formed up on the road, because forming up on the road was the only thing they knew how to do. It did not matter. It was never going to matter.

---

He saw the thing that broke them before he understood what he was seeing.

A single figure walked ahead of the line, unhurried, and where it turned its eyes, men fell — not struck, not shot, simply *undone*, dropping their spears to claw at their own ears, their own eyes, as though the air itself had turned against them. Klein had heard the word for it in a lecture hall, spoken the way you speak of weather in some other country.

*Sense.*

He had not quite believed it was real. It was real. It walked through the garrison of Corneo like a man wading through tall grass.

---

After that, it was only noise.

The line broke. Some ran, and were cut down on the road. Some threw down their arms and were taken. Some were simply not there anymore — the way people are not there, in the parts of a battle you do not survive to remember. The town caught from its western edge and burned inward. Klein lost the shape of it. Lost the road. Lost, for one long moment, the whole reason a man is supposed to keep moving.

Then Aldric had him by the collar, hauling him up, shouting something with Klein's name in it and the word *trees* — and the two of them were running. Away from the road. Away from the fire. Away from the town that would not be called Corneo much longer. Into the pines. Into the fog. Into the only dark still willing to hide them.

---

Behind them, Corneo burned.

Ahead of them, the border they had come here to guard lay a long, dark forest away — and everything he had meant to go home to lay beyond it.

They ran.
