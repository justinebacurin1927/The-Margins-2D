# Lore System — Storyline Roadmap

Region: [[Region 1 - Forest]] (Corneo / occupied Herois, ~Year 408–418)
Related: [[Protagonist - Klein]] · [[World Structure System]] · [[NPC System]] · [[Debuff System]] · [[Day & Night Cycle System]] · [[Companion System]] · [[Dialog System]]

## Overview
The main-story spine for The Margin. Klein — a young Novelborne knight — lives through the Evermove conquest of Herois, ~50–60 years before the events of the main-story canon (Year 468). This roadmap locks the arc's backbone (Acts 0–2) and the canonical ending: Klein's ultimate goal is **GET HOME** — reach the Novelborne mainland, Magdalene, and his parents (see [[Protagonist - Klein]]). The two un-chosen purposes (the Ant, the Truth) become the first player-decided **"What if…"** branch endings.

---

## Structural Principle — Escalation, Not Descent

One continuous life, permadeath = the end, **no floor-descent** (per locked design). The story cannot ramp by going *deeper*. Instead:

> **The occupation tightens around a persistent Herois.** The map is one forest. Over the arc, it gets more hostile — more Gilimans patrols, curfews, hunts, harsher scarcity, worse weather season — while Klein grows more capable and learns more truth. Difficulty and story advance through **world-state escalation**, not new floors.

**What throttles the acts: quests.** Main-story **quests** (see [[Quest System]]) are the escalation gate — completing an act's key quest(s) tips the world into the next phase (the occupation tightens, new locations/threats open, the story moves). Survival is the constant background pressure; quests are the throttle that paces the narrative against it.

The three narrative acts map cleanly onto the existing danger tiers in [[World Structure System]]:

| Act | Klein's driver | Danger tier foregrounded | Locations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act 1 | **Survive** | Tier 1 (+ Kitchen Camp) | Hunter's Blind, Fallen Log Hollow, Forest Shrine, Beehive Grove, Worn Down Kitchen Camp |
| Act 2 | **Understand** | Tier 2 | Collapsed Watchtower, Poacher's Camp, Sunken Well |
| Act 3 | **Decide** | Tier 3 | The Old House, Mercenary Graveyard, Deep Cave Mouth |

## Narrative Architecture — One Spine, Branching Later *(LOCKED)*

The story is built in two layers, in this order:

1. **The Spine (build first — canonical).** ONE authored ultimate goal for Klein, with ONE true ending. This is *the* story of The Margin — the canonical fate. Focused, deep, fully authored. The whole game is designed and built around this first.
2. **The "What if…" Expansion (build later — player-decided).** Once the spine is solid, it branches: the player's choices across a life can divert Klein toward alternate endings — *"What if Klein had chosen differently?"* These are speculative divergences off the canonical path, not co-equal main stories. They add replay and agency without diluting the one true spine.

**Design rule:** *author for the canon, architect for the branch.* Everything (Companion, Dialog, choices, flags) serves the canonical spine first, but is structured so alternate endings can hang off it later without a rewrite. The SPD-style permadeath/replay loop is what carries this — each life can reach toward a different "What if."

**No waste:** of the three candidate purposes (Get Home / Become the Ant / The Buried Truth), the **one chosen is the canonical spine**; the **other two become the first "What if…" branch endings.**

## Act 0 — The Last Ordinary Day *(text intro — read or skip)*

**Delivery:** Act 0 is a **text-based intro, not gameplay** — a readable, **skippable** narrative sequence (fits the SPD-style, text-forward tone). It tells the *before* and *the fall*; the player can sit with the wound or skip straight into play.

**What the intro tells:**
- **The before:** Corneo under Novelborne, Klein's routine posting (guard the Copper Road + the town), **Magdalene's letter** — the ordinary life about to end.
- **The fall:** Evermove invades; **Sense-users** shatter the defense (the "elephant made flesh"); the Novelborne **Lamilla** and garrison try to hold and fail; the **Gilimans** take Corneo; comrades desert, are killed, are broken.

**Where gameplay begins:** Klein **spawns already in the Herois forest, mid-flight — and he's not alone.** **Aldric**, a fellow knight who survived the fall, flees at his side. No playable "morning"; the game opens *in motion, in danger,* with **run and hide** as the first beat.

**The tutorial — Aldric teaches it.** Running beside Klein, Aldric delivers the **how-to-play diegetically**: controls, hiding, the survival rules, the forest's dangers — a guide who *belongs in the fiction* instead of a wall of tooltips. (This resolves the onboarding-in-motion problem.)

**The hand-off — Aldric is taken.** The moment the player clears the how-to-play, **the chasers catch Aldric.** Klein escapes; his teacher and only comrade does not. It's the wound that opens Act 1 — and it plants a future **rescue-Aldric quest** (getting him back becomes one of Klein's threads). *The man who taught you to survive is the first thing the war takes.*

**End state → Act 1:** Klein alone, hunted, in the forest. Corneo will be renamed **Coneros**. Drives: *survive + understand.*

## Act 1 — Ash and Pine *(Survive)*

**Situation:** Klein is prey in a forest he doesn't know, in a kingdom that just became enemy ground.

- **The survival loop takes over.** Hunger, Thirst, Temperature, Weather, spoilage, first debuffs (raw meat / mushrooms). He learns to forage, cook (Coal), purify water, repair gear. Tier 1 locations are his classroom.
- **Noble made nobody.** His rank, name, and blood are worthless to the Gilimans — he's just another survivor to hunt. The story's emotional floor: a man who always had status, dropped to the bottom of a foreign ladder (ties to [[Status System]]).
- **He is not alone.** Other survivors, deserters, and refugees haunt the forest. First contact with the two traders ([[NPC System]] — the Traveling Wanderer, the Caravan Black Market Trader). First hints that the forest has its own factions.
- **Beat:** the shock wears into grim routine. Klein stops being a knight who lost a battle and becomes a man trying to see tomorrow.

**Revelation seeds:** found journals; the presence of a **mercenary company** in the woods (see Act 2); the Gilimans are settling in, not passing through — this is conquest, not a raid.

---

## Act 2 — What the Fog Hides *(Understand)*

**Situation:** Klein starts pulling threads. The "understand" drive matures from *what happened to me* into *what is actually happening here.*

- **The conquest's shape.** Not a border skirmish — a full annexation. Novelborne has lost/abandoned Herois. Klein learns how the Sense-users broke his side, and that Evermove's reach is deliberate and total.
- **The mercenary company, alive.** The company tied to the [[World Structure System|Collapsed Watchtower → Kitchen Camp → Graveyard]] loop is a **present-day faction in Klein's era** — hired blades in the Herois war (Valens' mercenary economy). Their Graveyard is being *filled now.* (In the main story 50 years later, this same company is ancient lore — Klein lives its end.)
- **The underground.** Herois is the kingdom's least-watched corner — thick with **Heretics** (old-faith faithful) and rumors of a hidden **Twilight Knight**, a fallen order hunted for a century. First real contact with the world's power systems (Sense / Mystic / Magic) as something more than a weapon pointed at him.
- **Companions enter.** Klein gathers allies — a fellow deserter, a refugee, a Heretic, a hardened local ([[Companion System]] + [[Dialog System]] live here). They carry threads of the world's truth and change how he can survive.
- **The fuse (distant).** A first, tantalizing thread of the buried truth — the Evermove king's 174-year "divinity" and the long-lived-subspecies secret. Klein only glimpses it; it is not this game's to resolve.

**Beat:** knowledge becomes a burden. Now that Klein understands, *understanding is no longer enough.* He has to decide what he's for. → **Act 3 branch point.**

## Act 3 — The Weight of a Name *(Decide)*

Klein's ultimate goal pays off here. In the **canonical spine**, Act 3 drives to the **one true ending** (the chosen purpose below). The Tier 3 locations (Old House, Mercenary Graveyard, Deep Cave Mouth) are the high-risk stages where it resolves. The two purposes *not* chosen become the first **"What if…" branch endings** in the later expansion (see Narrative Architecture). Candidate spines — pick ONE as canon:

### ★ Branch A — Get Home *(CANONICAL — LOCKED)*
The arc becomes a **journey across the Herois frontier forest** to the **northwest border crossing** into Novelborne (see [[Herois Region]]). Canon places Coneros as a NW border town at the treeline — so **home is only a few miles of forest away**, and the invasion turns those few miles into the deadliest journey of Klein's life. Evermove/Giliman forces sweep in from the **east** (down the Copper Road); safety is **west**, across the border. The forest is the gauntlet; the **NW border crossing** (not the Deep Cave Mouth) is the way out. The most human goal against the most inhuman backdrop: the ant just wants to go home. **The ending (LOCKED): he makes it home** — Klein crosses into Novelborne and, in the epilogue beyond the playable region, reaches the mainland, Magdalene, and the life the war tried to erase. An *earned* homecoming, hard-won across a lost war. (Texture still open: how changed he arrives, and whether the home he returns to is untouched.)

### Branch B — Become the Ant *("What if…" branch — later)*
Klein **awakens a Sense ability** (at the cost of the Sensory Burden — permanent, escalating). Prey becomes threat. The arc turns to **resistance / vengeance** against the occupiers, off the setting's epigraph (*"an ant that killed many elephants"*). His stand could plant the seed of a Herois underground that echoes into the main story. Ending question: what does power cost a man who was never meant to have it?

### Branch C — The Buried Truth *("What if…" branch — later)*
Klein's "understand" drive goes **all the way up** — chasing the king's divinity secret through the war's wreckage. An investigation/conspiracy arc ending with Klein carrying a truth too dangerous to hold. Ending question: is a truth worth more than a life, when no one will believe it?

**Canon vs. What-if:** whichever ONE is chosen becomes the authored canonical ending (built first). The other two are seeded now and expanded later as player-decided "What if…" divergences. A blend is also possible for the canon (e.g., "get home, but only after learning what he can never un-know").

## Epilogue — Legacy *(ties to Year 468 canon)*

As a prequel, the finale plants seeds that pay off 50–60 years later in the main story:
- **Corneo is now Coneros** — the renamed, occupied town Milek is born in.
- **The Mercenary Graveyard** — the ancient lore loop of the main story is the grave Klein watched being dug.
- **The occupation** — the Gilimans, the poverty, the fog and pines of Herois that shape Milek's world all begin here.
- **The underground** — whatever Klein does with the Heretics / Twilight remnant can seed the hidden faithful still alive in 468.
- **Klein's own fate** — one of the Act 3 branch resolutions becomes his canonical end; the Herois he leaves behind *is* the setting of The Margins.

## Threat / World-State Escalation (the ramp)

| Phase | Occupation pressure | Environment | Scarcity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early (Act 1) | Gilimans consolidating; sparse patrols | Milder season | Forage-able; risk from bad food |
| Mid (Act 2) | Patrols + curfews; mercenaries hunting; bounties | Worsening weather, longer nights | Competition for loot; traders matter |
| Late (Act 3) | Systematic sweeps; the forest is a trap closing | Harshest conditions (Cold Snap era / storms) | Everything contested; every choice costs |
